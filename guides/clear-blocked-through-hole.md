# Clear a Blocked Through-Hole

Use this method after the component is removed, when pumping or wicking has left a solder plug in the hole. A needle holds a passage open while the solder cools.

## Tools

- Temperature-controlled iron, electronics flux, and fresh solder
- Smooth, clean stainless-steel desoldering needle, preferably blunt
- PCB holder, tweezers or pliers, and good lighting or a magnifier
- Compatible flux cleaner and a swab, if needed

Choose a needle that clears the copper barrel without scraping it and leaves an opening large enough for the replacement lead. Rough or solder-wetted needles can stick.

Disconnect power and batteries and address stored capacitor charge. Wear eye protection, ventilate fumes, and hold the hot needle with tweezers or pliers. Never force it through solid solder or use it on a loose pad or damaged barrel.

## Steps

1. **Inspect and secure the board.** Check both sides of the empty hole for lifted pads, damaged copper, and nearby heat-sensitive parts. Clamp the PCB gently. Stop if the pad or barrel is coming loose.

2. **Add flux and fresh solder.** Apply flux and melt a little fresh solder onto the pad to improve thermal contact with the old plug.

3. **Melt the whole plug.** Use a clean, suitably sized iron tip. Solder must be liquid through the board thickness, not just at the surface. If it will not melt promptly, improve tip contact or pause and reassess. Large copper planes may need more thermal capacity or controlled preheating; avoid prolonged heating with an undersized tip.

4. **Insert the needle.** While keeping the solder molten, gently slide the needle straight through the centre of the hole. If it meets resistance, withdraw it and check whether the plug is fully molten and the needle fits. Let the board cool if needed before reflowing and trying again.

5. **Let the solder harden.** Remove the iron and hold the needle still while the solder cools and solidifies around it. Leave the needle in place until the solder has hardened.

6. **Withdraw the needle.** Pull it gently straight out, without twisting or yanking. If it sticks, reheat the solder to release it, then reassess the needle's material, surface, and size.

7. **Check the opening and clean.** Inspect with a light or magnifier. The replacement lead should fit without force; some solder can remain on the barrel. If the opening is too small, use a suitable needle in a separate controlled reflow operation. Do not drill through the copper plating. After cooling, clean compatible flux residue and inspect the pad and barrel before soldering the replacement.

Stop if the board discolours, a pad moves, or repeated attempts fail. A temperature-controlled vacuum tool or professional rework may be more suitable.
